# Optional screenshots and demonstration media

Capture before images before changing anything. If the user already supplied originals, preserve them as historical evidence with clear dates. Never reconstruct a tidy current layout and label it an authentic before image.

Capture after images from the completed phone. Do not substitute the USB wallpaper-preview API: the tested output included placeholder widgets and icon styling that differed from the live screen. Use matching crops, scale and background for comparisons. Remove unrelated desktop content and inspect for private messages, names, badges, account details and personal photos before sharing. Do not bundle a user's images with the reusable skill.

For a landscape loop showing a portrait phone, a useful composition is:
- Full phone on the left.
- A magnified crop of the same recorded action on the right.
- Both panels exactly the same visible height.
- White or neutral background as requested.
- No title cards, opening or closing slates unless wanted.

Crop each source to the needed aspect ratio before scaling. Stretching a crop distorts the icons. Keep pointer movement and the action visible. Use genuine recordings of a few reversible icon moves, and restore the final layout. Never portray a simulation as a recording of the actual phone.

With FFmpeg, trim the useful action, normalize frame rate/timestamps, split into full/detail views, crop each, scale to equal heights and overlay on the landscape canvas. Use a short end-to-start crossfade only where the frames permit it. Verify the loop seam, actual duration, dimensions and several frames from the exported file. MP4/H.264 with yuv420p and faststart is a common delivery choice.

Verify current publishing capabilities and dimensions for the precise destination. A LinkedIn article cover, feed video, newsletter image and profile banner are different surfaces; do not promise that a video can be an article header. A still cover plus an in-article or companion video may be necessary.

Draft captions or handoff messages separately. Sharing, uploading or messaging another assistant requires the user's applicable authorization. A local export does not imply permission to publish.
